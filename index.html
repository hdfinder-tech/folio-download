Folio

A reader for PDF, EPUB and Word files on the Mac.

Download Folio For any Mac with macOS 13 (Ventura) or later.

Opening it

  1. Double-click the downloaded Folio.zip, and Folio appears beside it, in Downloads. (Safari may already have done this for you.)
  2. Double-click Folio. Your Mac says it was downloaded from the internet; click Open. That’s the only time it asks.
  3. Add your books with Add Files…, or drag them into the sidebar.

Folio can live in any folder, even Downloads; it doesn’t need Applications. To keep it in the Dock, move it once first (into Documents, say) and open it from there.

Folio is signed by its developer and checked by Apple (notarized).

What it does

  • Reads PDFs, EPUBs and Word documents, and reopens each where you left off.
  • Search for several words and Folio lists first the places where they appear exactly as you typed them, then the places where they all come up close together, in any order. Accents and capitals don’t matter. To see only the exact phrase, put it in quotes.
  • Search All Books looks through the text of every book in your library at once and lists what it finds book by book: type in the box above the library and press Return. The first time, Folio reads each book once, which takes a few minutes; after that a search takes a second or two.
  • Finds related passages: places close in meaning to what you search for, even in other words. It works in some fifty languages, among them English, French, German and Spanish, and across them: a question in English can find the answer in a French book.
  • Reads the words of scanned pages in English, French, German, Spanish, Italian and Portuguese, so related passages can be found in them too.
  • Copies from a PDF as clean paragraphs, with words broken across lines joined again.

Search by meaning runs entirely on your Mac. Nothing you read or search for is sent anywhere.

About 440 MB, most of it the language model behind related passages. Runs natively on Apple-chip and Intel Macs.
